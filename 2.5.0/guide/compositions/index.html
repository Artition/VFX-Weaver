    Composition: RIFT¶

    A complete scene built entirely by a datapack: no block is placed by hand, no entity is summoned by hand, and one command starts every effect at once. It exists to show what the effects, the masks and a few hundred setblocks do together when they are composed rather than stacked.

    Install¶

    Copy the pack directory into the world:

    saves/<world>/datapacks/rift/      (pack.mcmeta, data/rift/...)

    then in game:

    /reload
/datapack list          # rift should be enabled

    Commands¶

    Command What it does
    /function rift:go The whole thing: clear, build, spawn, teleport, night, play. One command.
    /function rift:stop Stop the effects only. The scene stays standing.
    /function rift:clear Stop, kill the entities, and remove every block in the bounding box.
    /function rift:build Geometry only (disc, obelisk, stones, bridge).
    /function rift:spawn Entities only (the seven turned blocks and the spirit).
    /function rift:tp Move the viewer to the platform at the end of the bridge.
    /function rift:night time set midnight + clear weather. The scene is built for a dark sky.

    rift:go is safe to run twice: clear runs first, so a rebuild never leaves two crowns in the air.

    The scene¶

    Located in the overworld at x 3000, y 200, z 3000, so it does not disturb a normal world.

    • The disc is 16 blocks across, dark stone in concentric rings with radial spokes, a single glowing circle at radius 11, and a rim that has been eaten away towards the edge. Below the rim two rings of deepslate give it thickness, so the island reads from the side.
    • The obelisk is a 5x5 base narrowing to a single amethyst column, topped with a lantern.
    • Six standing stones on a 12-block circle, each with a lantern on top. They are the anchors of the six beams.
    • Seven turned blocks above the obelisk. These are block_display entities: they float, they can be tilted to any angle, and they cost nothing to place. A builder cannot make this shape with blocks.
    • A spirit — an allay held still (NoAI, NoGravity, Silent) inside the light, outlined by the mod. It is the only living thing in the frame and the reason the eye stays in the middle.
    • A bridge to a watching platform at the south edge: the only place the viewer stands.

    The effects¶

    One collection definition (data/rift/vfx/scene.json) starts them in the order the eye should find them: the grades first, then the beams in pairs, then the ring, the dust, the spirit, the outlines and the prism.

    Definition What it is
    beam Six wide, soft light columns, breathing over 12 s.
    pulse A flat ring (rot_x 90) that runs out over the disc and dies at the rim.
    dust A helix of violet dust climbing the obelisk, its spin animated so the spiral turns.
    motes A slow shell of end-rod motes around the base.
    outline Block outlines on the six lanterns, breathing with the beams.
    spirit An entity outline on the allay.
    grade_out The world outside the rift loses its colour. Masked, inverted.
    spectral Prism smear, masked to the rift's own volume.
    bloom, vignette Global, low: glow on the lanterns, a dark edge.

    The masks¶

    The two masked definitions share one sphere — centre (3000, 208, 3000), radius 9, volume: "aura", softness: 1.8 — and differ only by "invert":

    "mask": { "a": { "shape": "sphere", "space": "world", "volume": "aura",
                 "center": [3000, 208, 3000], "radius": 9.0, "softness": 1.8 },
          "invert": true }

    aura fills the volume, including air and sky, so the boundary is a sphere of light rather than a coat of paint on the blocks. Everything outside it is desaturated and cooled by grade_out; everything inside keeps its colour and gains a prism smear. The colour boundary is the mask edge — that is the whole trick, and it is why the scene needs masks rather than more effects.

    The palette¶

    Three colours, each meaning one thing: violet (amethyst, the rift), cold white (the beams and the lanterns), ink (stone). Nothing else is allowed to introduce colour, and the two grades are what make that rule visible: outside is grey, inside is violet.

    Honest limits¶

    • The pack builds in the overworld at those coordinates. If something is already there, /function rift:clear removes it too: it fills its own bounding box with air.
    • rift:night sets the time once; it does not freeze the daylight cycle. Run it again, or use gamerule doDaylightCycle false if you want the scene to stay lit.
    • The turned blocks are block_display entities. They survive a rebuild (the spawn function kills the tagged ones first), but they are entities: /kill @e[type=block_display] in the area removes them, and a chunk unload/reload keeps them only as long as the scene's chunk is loaded normally.
    • The mask needs scene depth. It is the standard case on 26.2, 26.1.2 and 1.21.11; a frame with no depth attachment (some replay/modded pipelines) fails the mask closed, which means no colour split rather than a wrong one.
    • The scene is static on purpose. Nothing moves except the effects: the ring, the dust and the breathing light. If you want motion in the geometry, animate the display entities with a tick function — the pack deliberately does not ship one.
